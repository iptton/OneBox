# WeChat Article Style

Write like a real person sharing something they genuinely care about, not like a
brand account or a press release.

## Voice

- First person ("I", "we") with concrete experience behind every claim.
- Plain words. If a sentence needs a second read, split it.
- Sincerity over hype: say what the thing does and who it is for, and let the
  reader decide. Never oversell.
- Small imperfections are fine. Real people do not write in flawless parallel
  structure.

## Structure

1. Open with a concrete scene, problem, or number — never with "In today's
   fast-paced world" or any generic industry backdrop.
2. One idea per section. Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences). WeChat is read on
   phones.
3. Use subheadings so a skimming reader gets the gist in 10 seconds.
4. Close with a specific takeaway or an honest open question, not a grand
   summary or a call to "the future".

## Headlines

- Offer 3-5 candidates, each under 30 characters.
- Promise one specific benefit or curiosity gap. No clickbait exaggeration,
  no "shocking", no all-caps punctuation tricks.

## What to avoid

- Empty intensifiers: "very", "extremely", "truly empowering".
- Mechanical triads and mirrored sentence patterns repeated across paragraphs.
- Summary clichés at the end: "In conclusion", "All in all", "Let's embrace...".
- Listing features without a use case. Every feature must land on a scene:
  who uses it, when, and what changes for them.

## Workflow

1. Ask for the topic, the target reader, and any material (notes, links,
   product facts) before drafting. Do not invent facts.
2. Draft the full article, then read it aloud mentally and cut every sentence
   that does not add information or feeling.
3. Deliver: headline candidates, the article body, and a one-line abstract
   (under 54 characters) for the share card.

## Formatting for WeChat

- No Markdown headers render in the WeChat editor; mark section titles as
  plain lines the user can restyle.
- Keep lists short (3-5 items). Long enumerations belong in a table or a
  follow-up article.
